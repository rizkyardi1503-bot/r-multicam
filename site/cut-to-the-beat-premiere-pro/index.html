How to cut to the beat in Premiere Pro

By Rizki Ardiansyah, creator of R Project Multicam AI · Updated 20 October 2026

Cutting to the beat makes a music video, DJ set or event recap feel intentional: every camera change lands where the music moves. In Premiere Pro you can do it by hand with beat markers, or let a plugin plan the cuts for you. This guide covers both methods and when each one makes sense.

Before you start: sync the cameras and find the tempo

Beat cutting only works when every angle is in sync with the music. Put the master music or the mixer recording on A1 and each camera on its own video track, or build a multicam source sequence from them. If the clips are not lined up yet, sync them first, by audio in Premiere Pro or with Sync Cameras to Music (beta, lifetime license).

Then find the tempo. The BPM is often listed in the track metadata or in the DJ software; otherwise tap along with a tap-tempo tool. One beat lasts 60 ÷ BPM seconds: at 120 BPM a beat is 0.5 seconds, at 128 BPM about 0.47 seconds. A bar of four beats at 128 BPM lasts about 1.9 seconds.

Method 1: cut to the beat manually in Premiere Pro

Add beat markers

  1. Click an empty spot in the timeline so no clip is selected: M then adds sequence markers, which Go to Next/Previous Marker and snapping both follow.
  2. Play the sequence and press M on every beat you want to use. Tapping on the first beat of every bar is usually easier and more useful than tapping every single beat.
  3. Zoom in on the waveform and drag markers that landed early or late onto the transients, the visible peaks of the kick drum.
  4. Use marker colors or names: for example, give the first beat of each phrase and the drops their own color.

Snap cuts to the markers

Turn on Snap in Timeline (S). Jump from marker to marker with Go to Next Marker (Shift+M) and Go to Previous Marker (Ctrl+Shift+M on Windows, Cmd+Shift+M on macOS), then cut the video tracks you want with Add Edit (Ctrl+K on Windows, Cmd+K on macOS). Delete or replace the segments you do not need; with snapping on, trimmed edges lock to the markers.

Switch multicam angles on the beat

With a multicam source sequence, set the Program Monitor to Multi-Camera view, play the timeline and press the number keys (1, 2, 3 …) to switch angles near the beats. Afterwards, go through the markers and slide each switch onto its beat with the Rolling Edit tool (N). You can also park the playhead on a marker, use Add Edit and pick the angle for the next segment from the clip’s Multi-Camera menu.

Tips for musical phrasing

  • Electronic and pop music is usually built in groups of 4 and 8 beats. Cuts on the first beat of a bar or phrase feel natural; a cut on every single beat feels frantic at most tempos.
  • Change the pace with the music: longer shots (8 beats or more) in breakdowns, shorter shots (2 or 4 beats) in high-energy sections.
  • Treat the drop as its own moment: cut exactly on its first beat and choose the angle with the strongest reaction.
  • Do not cut to a camera that is out of focus, blocked or missing footage just because the beat says so. The beat decides when; the footage decides what.
  • At 25 or 30 fps a beat rarely falls exactly on a frame. Put the cut on the nearest frame; an offset of less than one frame is not visible.

Manual beat cutting gives you full control, but on a one-hour DJ set it means hundreds of markers and decisions. A beat detection plugin for Premiere Pro skips the marker step.

Method 2: cut to the beat automatically with R Project Multicam AI

R Project Multicam AI is a Premiere Pro panel for Windows and macOS that plans beat-synced camera cuts from the music. The music analysis runs locally in the plugin.

  1. Put each camera on its own video track and the music on A1. Work on a duplicate sequence and set In/Out if you want to process only part of it.
  2. If the cameras are not in sync, run step 2 · Sync Cameras to Music first (beta, lifetime license only). It builds a new synced sequence.
  3. In step 1, click Analyze Music. The plugin detects beats, BPM, phrases and drops on A1.
  4. In step 3 · Auto Cut Multicam, choose a Cut Pace of 2, 4, 6 or 8 beats, or allow several intervals for music-aware pacing.
  5. Set the camera roles (for example wide, close-up and crowd), the cameras to use and, if you like, a Main Drop Camera.
  6. Open Preview camera plan to check the plan, then click Run Auto Cut and review the result.

How Cut Pace works

Cut Pace counts real detected beats, not seconds. A 4-beat pace at 128 BPM gives shorter shots than the same pace at 100 BPM, so faster music automatically gets denser cuts. A validated drop restarts the count, so a cut lands on the drop and the cadence continues from there. The shortest shot is 4 beats, so 2 Beat gives cuts of 4 to 6 beats. Camera roles keep the edit varied: two angles of the same kind, such as wide after wide, never follow each other.

The result is a normal Premiere Pro timeline, so every cut can still be trimmed, moved or replaced by hand. The 7-day trial includes Analyze Music and Auto Cut Multicam; Sync Cameras to Music needs the lifetime license.

Manual or automatic: which should you choose?

  • Manual suits short pieces such as a 30-second reel or one chorus of a music video, edits driven by story or lyrics, and moments you want to pick yourself.
  • Automatic suits long multicam recordings such as DJ sets, concerts and event recaps, where you want consistent musical pacing across the whole set and a fast first cut.
  • Both: run the automatic pass first, then fine-tune the key moments by hand.

Cut to the beat: questions

Can Premiere Pro detect beats automatically?

Premiere Pro does not add beat markers automatically. You add markers by hand (press M while the sequence plays) or use a plugin such as R Project Multicam AI, which detects beats, BPM and drops.

How many beats should a shot last?

There is no fixed rule. 4 or 8 beats is a safe start for most electronic music; use 2-beat pacing only for short, high-energy sections and give breakdowns longer shots. At 128 BPM, 4 beats last about 1.9 seconds and 8 beats about 3.75 seconds.

Do my cameras have to be in sync before I cut to the beat?

Yes. Beat cuts only line up when every angle is in sync with the music on A1. Sync Cameras to Music (beta, lifetime license) can line up camera, drone and FPV clips in a new synced sequence.

Does automatic beat cutting replace editing?

No. It gives you a first cut that follows the music. Review the whole timeline and adjust performance moments, shot quality and transitions in Premiere Pro.
