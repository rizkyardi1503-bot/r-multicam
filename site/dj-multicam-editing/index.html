DJ Set & Concert Multicam Editing in Premiere Pro

To edit a multi-camera DJ set in Premiere Pro, put each camera on its own video track and the cleanest recording of the mix on A1, then sync every camera to that master audio. Find the beats, build-ups and drops, and switch cameras on the beat with a strong angle on every drop. R Project Multicam AI does the sync, the music analysis and the beat-timed camera switches inside Premiere Pro, but you still review the whole edit before delivery.

How to edit a DJ set video with multiple cameras

The order is the same for a club night, a festival stage or a concert.

  1. Record with the edit in mind. Let every camera record its own audio, even if it never reaches the final mix: the sync listens to it. Get a direct recording from the mixer and set the camera and drone clocks before the event.
  2. Build the timeline. Put each angle on its own video track and the mixer recording on A1. Work on a duplicate sequence so the original stays untouched.
  3. Sync the cameras. If the angles are not lined up yet, sync every camera to the master music with step 2 · Sync Cameras to Music (beta). It builds a new synced sequence and puts unsure clips on a muted Review track.
  4. Map the music. Click Analyze Music in step 1 and check BPM, Drops, Energy and Drop score before the first cut. The beat detection plugin for Premiere Pro page explains how they are found.
  5. Plan the switches. In step 3 · Auto Cut Multicam, choose a Cut Pace, the camera roles and a Main Drop Camera, then open Preview camera plan before applying. Nothing changes on the timeline until you apply.
  6. Apply, then review. Click Run Auto Cut on a short In/Out range first, then on the set. The result is a normal Premiere Pro timeline you can keep editing by hand.

Prefer to cut by hand? The same order works with the multicam tools built into Premiere Pro: see how to edit multicam in Premiere Pro step by step.

Cut to the build-up, drop and breakdown

DJ sets move in waves: tension builds, a drop releases it and a breakdown lets the room breathe. Make that shape visible instead of switching at a steady rate.

  • Build-up: establish the venue and the performer with steady, medium-length shots, and save your most energetic angle for the drop.
  • Drop: switch exactly on the impact, to the strongest angle with footage at that moment, often the crowd or a wide stage shot as the lights hit.
  • Breakdown: slow down, hold the DJ close-up or the wide longer, and keep strong performance moments by hand.

In the plugin, a validated main drop forces a camera change where the music hits, and the beat count restarts from there. Choose the angle under Main Drop Camera or leave it on Auto; if a detected drop is wrong, open Choose the main drops and pick your own. Since v2.3.11.14, Analyze Music also finds bars per section, fixes tempo misreads in breakdowns and follows tempo changes.

Coverage has the last word: if the drop angle has no footage at that moment, the edit uses another enabled camera, and if a camera’s footage ends inside a shot, it switches to the camera that continues.

Camera roles for DJ sets and concerts

Give every camera one job and use only angles that add something. The Druva edit above cuts between five: booth, DJ close-up, DJ wide, stage and crowd.

Wide stage

Venue, production and crowd scale. Safe for drops and long holds.

DJ close-up

Hands, expression and reactions. Carries build-ups and breakdowns.

Medium front

Artist and booth in one frame.

Behind the DJ

The crowd from the performer’s side. Strong on drops.

Crowd or roaming

Energy and faces, when the shot is steady.

Deck and detail

Faders, jog wheels and hands. Accents for busy sections.

In step 3, set each camera’s role (shot size and direction): two angles of the same kind, such as wide after wide, never follow each other, and a cut is only made when the camera changes. Since v2.3.11.9, the AI director also checks each camera for brightness, sharpness and shake and avoids dark, blurry or shaky moments. Make sure every selected camera has footage through the range you edit.

Pace the cuts by energy

Cut Pace sets how many beats pass between camera decisions: 2, 4, 6 or 8, or several intervals for music-aware pacing. It counts real beats, so faster music gets denser cuts at the same pace, and since v2.3.11.7 cuts stay on the beat when the next song starts. The shortest shot is 4 beats, so 2 Beat gives cuts of 4 to 6 beats.

A starting point by energy, not a promise of which shots the plugin picks:

  • Intro and build-up: 4 beats, so viewers can read each angle.
  • Drop and peak time: 2 or 4 beats, when every angle has usable footage.
  • Breakdown: 8 beats, or a longer hold placed by hand.

Duration in seconds = beats × 60 ÷ BPM: at 128 BPM, 4 beats last about 1.9 seconds and 8 beats 3.75 seconds. These are decision points, not guaranteed cuts; if the same camera wins again, the shot continues. The Editing presets are quick starts too: Concert uses 2/4 beats, Event 4/6/8 and Wedding 6/8. To place beat cuts by hand, see how to cut to the beat in Premiere Pro.

Long DJ sets: edit them in sections

A full club or festival set is long enough that small mistakes hide easily, so treat it as a series of short edits:

  • Test on a duplicate. Duplicate the sequence, set a 30-second In/Out range with a build-up and a drop, and compare 4 Beat with 2 and 8 before choosing the pace for the set.
  • Work in sections. Set In/Out around one stretch at a time, such as the opening, the first peak or the closing song. The plugin processes the range you set and respects it when applying a plan.
  • Keep a way back. Save first. The backup copy before Apply is on by default, and Apply · Keep Clips keeps the unused camera pieces.
  • Resume, don’t restart. If Auto Cut stops, Continue Multicam in step 3 resumes the saved job, even after the project is reopened. Do not change the sequence or A1 first.
  • Mind the memory. Use proxies for long or high-resolution projects and close other heavy apps. On Macs with 12 GB RAM or less, Low Memory mode turns on automatically.
  • Review in passes. One section at a time, one thing per pass: sync, drops, performance moments, then picture and sound.

Since v2.3.11.9, music engine V19 follows one continuous beat chain through the whole song, so cuts stay on the beat on long DJ sets.

When not to use automatic cuts

Cut or fix by hand in these cases:

  • Speech-led moments, such as an MC, a toast or an interview: the plugin switches cameras by music, not by who is talking.
  • A moment that matters more than the beat, such as a guest on stage. Beat timing alone does not decide which shot tells the story.
  • Cameras that are not in sync with the music on A1: cuts only line up when every camera is synced.
  • No readable music on A1, or an analysis that looks wrong. There is no accuracy guarantee, so check BPM and drops first.
  • Only one camera with footage: Auto Cut Multicam stops before any change and names the tracks to use.

Even when everything fits, the result still needs a full review before delivery.

What to check before delivery

Automatic editing is a starting point, not a replacement for editorial judgment. Before export, check:

  • Sync: hands, lips and crowd jumps match the music. After Sync Cameras to Music, look at the [CHECK] clips, the muted Review track and the [CLOCK] track.
  • Drops: each main drop switches on the impact, not a beat late.
  • Coverage and flow: no empty tracks or black frames, key moments held long enough, and nothing that feels like a mechanical V1 → V2 → V3 loop.
  • Picture: exposure and color match across cameras. Step 6 · Color LUT puts one LUT on all footage (needs Premiere Pro 2026 or later), but matching cameras is your job.
  • Sound and export: the master mix plays clean, camera audio is muted or mixed on purpose, and a short test export looks right on the target screen, such as a phone for a vertical reel.

DJ multicam editing questions

How do I edit a multicam DJ set automatically in Premiere Pro?

Put each angle on a separate video track and the master music on A1. If the cameras are not in sync yet, use Sync Cameras to Music first. Click Analyze Music, choose Cut Pace and the cameras in Auto Cut Multicam, then click Run Auto Cut. Use a duplicated sequence for your first test.

Can camera changes follow music beats rather than speech?

Yes. The current R Project workflow uses music timing and structure, not speaker tracking. It is designed for DJ sets, concerts and music-led event footage.

Will the plugin finish a live-set video without review?

No. Review the complete edit for synchronization, shot quality, footage gaps and performance moments. Color matching, audio mixing and final delivery remain part of your Premiere Pro workflow.

How do I edit a 90-minute DJ set?

Like a short one, in sections. On a duplicate sequence, test a 30-second In/Out range with a build-up and a drop, choose the pace, then run the set whole or one section at a time. If Auto Cut stops, Continue Multicam in step 3 resumes the saved job.

Can I edit a DJ set with only two cameras?

Yes, as long as both have footage in the range you cut; with only one, Auto Cut Multicam stops before any change. Give them different roles, such as a wide and a DJ close-up, because two angles of the same kind never follow each other. Where one camera has no footage, the edit stays on the other.

Which audio should I use as the master on A1?

The cleanest recording of the music you have, ideally a direct recording from the mixer or the board mix. Analyze Music reads it on A1, and Sync Cameras to Music places each camera clip against it by the clip’s own audio, so record audio on every camera too. If the master covers only part of the shoot, a camera clip placed with high confidence lends its audio past the end of the music (since v2.3.11.18), so the others can still sync.
