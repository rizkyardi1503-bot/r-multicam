Music-driven multicam editing in Premiere Pro

By Rizki Ardiansyah, creator of R Project Multicam AI · Updated October 18, 2026

A live set or event edit needs both musical timing and useful camera angles. R Project Multicam AI analyzes your music and uses that map to plan camera switches in Adobe Premiere Pro.

1. Prepare synchronized footage

Put each camera on its own video track and the music on A1, then select the sequence or set In/Out for an edit range. Check footage coverage and save your project. Cameras not in sync yet? Step 2 in the panel, Sync Cameras to Music, lines up every camera, drone and FPV clip and builds a new synced sequence (beta, lifetime license only).

2. Analyze the music

Click Analyze Music in step 1. Local FFmpeg/DSP analysis finds beats, BPM, phrases, energy and drop candidates, and shows BPM, Drops, Energy and Drop score. Open Choose the main drops if you want to pick them yourself. Auto Cut Multicam and Best Drops reuse this map while the sequence and A1 stay unchanged.

3. Choose cameras and pacing

In step 3 · Auto Cut Multicam, pick an Editing preset or set Cut Pace yourself: 2, 4, 6 or 8 beats, or several intervals for music-aware pacing. Cut Pace counts real beats, and a validated main drop restarts the count. The shortest shot is 4 beats, so 2 Beat gives cuts of 4 to 6 beats. Choose a Main Drop Camera and the cameras to use under Camera roles & cameras used. To compare this with marking beats by hand, see how to cut to the beat in Premiere Pro.

4. Build camera switches

Open Preview camera plan before applying if you want to check the plan first. Tick Save backup copy of sequence before applying, then click Run Auto Cut. A physical cut is made only when the selected camera changes; same-camera decisions remain continuous. If an angle has no footage coverage, another enabled, covered camera is used; if none is available, the original footage is preserved. If a camera’s footage ends inside a shot, the edit switches to the camera that continues. If a job stops, Continue Multicam lets you resume it.

5. Review the edit

Watch the entire result before delivery. Check transitions, camera choices and music timing. Then use step 4 · Best Drops: click Find Best Drops and render short highlight clips around the strongest drops. Step 5 · Random Zoom is optional: click Add Random Zoom after Auto Cut is finished. It replaces Scale keyframes in the processed range, so review existing motion first.

Music-driven editing for weddings, concerts and events

For a wedding highlight, start with slower 6/8-beat camera decisions to give moments room to breathe. Concert edits can start with 2/4 beats; an event recap can use 4/6/8 beats. These presets are starting points, so review camera coverage and the final edit before delivery.

Automatic cuts and missing camera coverage

Automatic multicam editing follows the analyzed music and your enabled camera roles. When the chosen camera has no footage at a decision point, the engine checks another covered camera. This coverage fallback helps avoid empty angles; it does not judge which performance or emotional moment is best. Apply · Keep Clips in the camera plan preview keeps unused pieces for a manual review.

Watch the 67-second multicam editing workflow to see the workflow before installing; your own edit runs in Premiere Pro during the 7-day trial.

What the plugin requires

Adobe Premiere Pro 2022 (v22) or newer is required and sold separately, on Windows or macOS. Music analysis runs locally in the plugin; internet is needed for sign-in, license checks and updates. The 7-day trial includes steps 1, 3, 4 and 5.
